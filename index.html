Legal Awareness & FAQs

Legal Knowledge & Citizen Rights

Informational guide covering common legal procedures in Punjab courts. Published in public interest to assist litigants with factual legal literacy.

Public Information Note: The information below is provided solely for educational and awareness purposes as permitted under the Bar Council of India guidelines. It does not constitute formal legal advice or create an advocate-client relationship.

In Punjab, when agricultural land is bought, inherited, or partitioned, a Mutation (Intiqal) must be entered into the revenue records by the Halqa Patwari and attested by the Revenue Officer (Tehsildar/Naib Tehsildar). If there is a dispute or contest regarding title or possession, the matter becomes a contested mutation and is decided following a summary inquiry.

Demarcation (Nishandehi) is the formal measurement of land boundaries carried out by the revenue staff using the authorized Musavi (revenue map) and total station/chain method to establish the exact physical boundaries on site.

Anticipatory Bail (under Section 438 CrPC / Section 482 BNSS) is sought by a person who apprehends arrest in a non-bailable offense. It is filed before the Court of Sessions or the High Court before an arrest takes place. If granted, the police must release the person on bail upon arrest.

Regular Bail (under Section 437/439 CrPC / Section 480/483 BNSS) is filed after the person has already been arrested or has surrendered before the competent court having judicial custody.

To initiate a criminal proceeding under Section 138 of the Negotiable Instruments Act for cheque dishonour:

  1. The cheque must be presented to the bank within its 3-month validity period.
  2. A statutory Legal Demand Notice must be issued to the drawer within 30 days of receiving the Bank Memo stating "Funds Insufficient" or other dishonour reason.
  3. The drawer is provided 15 days from receipt of notice to make payment.
  4. If the amount is not paid within those 15 days, a criminal complaint must be filed in the competent Court of Judicial Magistrate First Class within one month thereafter.

Under Order 39 Rules 1 & 2 of the Code of Civil Procedure (CPC), a temporary injunction (stay order) may be granted to restrain the opposite party from alienating, selling, or disturbing possession of the property. The applicant must satisfy three cardinal principles:

  • Prima Facie Case: A strong arguable case in favor of the plaintiff based on documented records.
  • Balance of Convenience: Proving that greater hardship will be caused if the injunction is refused than if it is granted.
  • Irreparable Injury: Establishing that monetary compensation would not sufficiently remedy the harm caused.

The New Civil Court Complex at Mukerian functions under the administrative jurisdiction of the District and Sessions Judge, Hoshiarpur (Punjab). It houses courts of the Civil Judge (Senior/Junior Division) and Judicial Magistrate First Class (JMFC), alongside sub-divisional administrative and revenue bodies.

Non-Resident Indians (NRIs) owning agricultural or ancestral land in Punjab often face unauthorized possession or fraudulent alienation attempts. Under Punjab state laws and specialized NRI fast-track provisions:

  • Special Summary Eviction: NRIs can invoke summary eviction procedures under the East Punjab Urban Rent Restriction Act and relevant revenue provisions for speedy recovery of possession.
  • Injunction Suits: A civil suit for permanent injunction can be filed restraining trespassers from changing the nature of the land or creating third-party rights.
  • Legal Representation via GPA/SPA: NRIs do not need to attend every court hearing in person; an advocate can represent them on the basis of a properly authenticated Special or General Power of Attorney registered through the Indian Embassy or Consulate.

To legally execute and register a Sale Deed (Vasiqa / Bainama) at the Sub-Registrar / Tehsil Office, Mukerian:

  1. Title & Revenue Record Verification: Detailed examination of the latest Jamabandi (Fard Malkiat), Intiqal (mutation record), and Non-Encumbrance clearance from the Revenue Department.
  2. Collector Rate Calculation: Calculating stamp duty and registration fees in accordance with the notified collector rates of Sub-Division Mukerian.
  3. Drafting & Execution: Precision drafting of the deed terms, consideration amount, boundaries (Chauhaddi), and terms of possession.
  4. Biometric & Witness Verification: Both buyer, seller, and two local witnesses appear before the Sub-Registrar for photo and biometric verification on the NGDRS portal.

When a family member passes away without leaving a registered Will:

For movable assets (such as bank accounts, fixed deposits, securities, shares, provident funds, or insurance), a petition under Section 372 of the Indian Succession Act is filed before the Civil Judge. The court issues a public citation in local newspapers inviting objections. If uncontested, a formal Succession Certificate is granted.

For immovable ancestral agricultural land, a legal heir mutation (Wirasat / Intiqal) is recorded by the Halqa Patwari based on genealogical tree (Shajra Nasab) verification and attested by the Revenue Officer.

Matters suitable for Lok Adalat include Section 138 Cheque Bounce cases, compoundable criminal matters, Motor Accident Claims (MACT), bank recovery, family settlements, and civil property disputes. Awards passed by the Lok Adalat have the statutory force of a civil court decree, are final and non-appealable, and any court fees previously paid are refunded to the parties.

Bail under the Narcotic Drugs and Psychotropic Substances (NDPS) Act depends fundamentally on the quantity recovered:

  • Small Quantity / Intermediate Quantity: Regular bail applications can be argued before the Special Court / Sessions Court at Hoshiarpur or High Court based on custody duration, absence of criminal antecedents, and trial delays.
  • Commercial Quantity: Governed by the stringent twin conditions under Section 37 of the NDPS Act. The defense must demonstrate reasonable grounds that the accused is not guilty and is unlikely to commit any offense while on bail.
  • Procedural Compliance: Rigorous examination of mandatory search and seizure procedures under Sections 42, 50, and 52-A of the NDPS Act.

Under Section 125 CrPC (Section 144 BNSS), a wife, minor children, and elderly parents unable to maintain themselves can claim monthly financial support from a person having sufficient means.

Courts routinely grant interim maintenance during the pendency of proceedings to prevent destitution. In addition, the Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act (PWDVA) provides comprehensive relief, including residence orders, protection against dispossession from the shared household, monetary compensation, and child custody orders.
